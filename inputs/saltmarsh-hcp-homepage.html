Boxed warning

Serious infections · Mortality · Malignancy · Major adverse cardiovascular events · Thrombosis

Serious infections. Patients treated with Tarnexa are at increased risk for developing serious infections that may lead to hospitalization or death. Most patients who developed these infections were taking concomitant immunosuppressants. If a serious infection develops, interrupt Tarnexa until the infection is controlled. Test for latent tuberculosis before and during therapy.

Mortality. In a large, randomized, postmarketing safety study of another JAK inhibitor in rheumatoid arthritis patients 50 years of age and older with at least one cardiovascular risk factor, a higher rate of all-cause mortality was observed compared with TNF blockers.

Malignancies. Lymphoma and other malignancies have been observed in patients treated with JAK inhibitors. Current or past smokers are at additional increased risk.

Major adverse cardiovascular events. Higher rates of MACE were observed with another JAK inhibitor compared with TNF blockers. Discontinue Tarnexa in patients who have experienced a myocardial infarction or stroke.

Thrombosis. Thrombosis, including pulmonary embolism, deep venous thrombosis and arterial thrombosis, has occurred in patients treated with JAK inhibitors. Avoid Tarnexa in patients at risk.

See full Prescribing Information, including Boxed Warning.

After a TNF inhibitor, a different mechanism.

Tarnexa is a once-daily oral selective JAK1 inhibitor indicated for adults with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more TNF inhibitors. Use of Tarnexa in combination with other JAK inhibitors, biologic DMARDs, or potent immunosuppressants is not recommended.

The ESTUARY program studied Tarnexa in the population you are treating: patients for whom at least one TNF inhibitor was not enough. Each step below adds detail; stop where your question is answered.

  1. What the pivotal trial showed

    Summary

    In ESTUARY-1, 58% of patients receiving Tarnexa 15 mg once daily plus background csDMARD achieved ACR20 at week 24 vs 29% with placebo plus csDMARD (p<0.001). ACR50 was achieved by 34% vs 13%.1

  2. Trial design and endpoints

    Pivotal data

    ESTUARY-1 was a 52-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial (N=612) in adults with moderately to severely active RA and an inadequate response to one or more TNF inhibitors, on stable background csDMARD. Primary endpoint: ACR20 at week 24. Key secondary endpoints: ACR50, ACR70, change in DAS28-CRP, change in HAQ-DI.1

  3. Patients who had failed two or more TNF inhibitors

    Prespecified subgroup

    In the prespecified subgroup with an inadequate response to two or more prior TNF inhibitors (n=203), ACR20 at week 24 was 51% with Tarnexa vs 24% with placebo. Response was consistent regardless of the reason for prior TNF discontinuation (primary non-response, secondary loss of response, or intolerance).5

    Subgroup analyses were not powered for formal hypothesis testing and are descriptive.

  4. Adverse reactions in the integrated safety population

    Safety tables

    Across ESTUARY-1, ESTUARY-2 and the long-term extension (1,004 patients, 1,612 patient-years), the most common adverse reactions (≥5%) were upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, headache and increased blood creatine phosphokinase. Serious infections occurred at 2.9 per 100 patient-years; herpes zoster at 3.4 per 100 patient-years.3,4

    See the Boxed Warning and the full Prescribing Information for warnings and precautions.

  5. Persistence after switching from a TNF inhibitor

    Real-world evidence

    In an interim analysis of the TIDELINE registry (1,280 patients initiating Tarnexa after a TNF inhibitor; 18 months), 71% remained on therapy at 12 months. Registry data are observational and subject to confounding; they do not establish efficacy.6

Selective for JAK1 in cellular assays.

Quilvecitinib preferentially inhibits JAK1 relative to JAK2, JAK3 and TYK2 in cellular assays. The clinical relevance of selectivity has not been established.7

Target
Janus kinase 1 (JAK1); modulates signaling of IL-6, interferons and γ-chain cytokines implicated in RA.
Dosing

Important Safety Information

Boxed warning

Serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events, and thrombosis. See the Boxed Warning above and the full Prescribing Information.

Hypersensitivity

Tarnexa is contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to quilvecitinib or any excipient. Angioedema and urticaria have been reported.

Gastrointestinal perforations

Use with caution in patients at increased risk, including those with a history of diverticulitis or taking NSAIDs or corticosteroids.

Laboratory abnormalities

Neutropenia, lymphopenia, anemia, lipid elevations and liver enzyme elevations have been observed. Monitor per the Prescribing Information; interrupt treatment as directed.

Vaccinations

Avoid live vaccines immediately before and during treatment. Update immunizations in agreement with current guidelines before initiating.

Embryo-fetal toxicity

Based on animal data, Tarnexa may cause fetal harm. Advise patients of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 weeks after the final dose.

Most common adverse reactions (≥5%)

Upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, headache, increased blood creatine phosphokinase.

Indication

Tarnexa is indicated for adults with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis who have had an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more TNF inhibitors. Use in combination with other JAK inhibitors, biologic DMARDs, or potent immunosuppressants is not recommended.
